Numbers with receipts.

Beyond the numbers

Stats are a proxy. Experience is the real signal.

Numbers are a useful shorthand, but the real indicator of a studio is the library of small decisions a seasoned team makes instinctively — a modal that dismisses right on touch, a form that recovers from a dropped connection, a color system that survives theme switching. No Lighthouse score will ever reveal those. So before hiring a studio on its stats, it helps to ask what the team believes makes a good website in the first place.

The task is the metric.

A good website is the one your customer finishes the task on. Beauty, speed, and feature lists are all in service of that. If nobody finishes, it’s a poster.

Speed dies under content.

A site that scores 98 empty can lose 30 points the moment a content team adds a carousel, a video, and a third-party chat widget. Real speed survives six months of marketing.

Accessibility is universal usability.

A site that scores 98 empty can lose 30 points the moment a content team adds a carousel, a video, and a third-party chat widget. Real speed survives six months of marketing.

Best Practices

Restraint is a design posture.

Design maturity shows in what’s not on the page. No slider you’ll ignore, no popup asking to chat, no footer stuffed with links you’ll never click. If you can cut it, cut it.
